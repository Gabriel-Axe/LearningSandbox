So, these last few days I have been... wondering, about what energy really is.

If you have read 1 or 2 posts previously (like in [[procrastination]] or in [[systems]]), you may know that I'm a bit of a fanatic for productivity, in a sense...

Here's what I currently know about motivation.

# It's not just 1 thing but rather 3

Generally when we talk about motivation, we refer to 1 of 3 things:
- Being excited towards doing something
- Having... uhum, motivation, to do something, a reason
- Being "able" to do it...

But using motivation as a umbrela term is not the most effective way to work towards productivity, since like I showed, it can be used in 3 extremelly different and non overlapping meanings.

# Energy

This fits the "being able to do something" prat of motivation.

In nature, energy is the capacity of something doing, the potential for it, and so it is for humans.

You can think of energy as the thing that cna change at what direction you are "going to".

Imagine a ball that is traveling through space at a speed of, lets say, x 30m/s, y 0m/s and z0m/s, and you apply energy to it, like fuel for example, making it so that it can change it's own velocity from 30, 0, 0 to 17, 14, 0.

A simple example, but it shows what energy really is.

Energy can help you do something, even when you don't feel like. It's the thing that can help you change your motion, that can save you from doomscrolling.

# Mood

this one fits the "being excited towards doing something" part of "motivation".

This is teh thing that any productivity guru will mean whatever they say "fuck motivation". What they mean is "fuck feeling good while doing it".

In a sense, I'll agree.

The problem is, your mood can both be affected and affect your energy, which explains why so many make motivation a umbrela term.

# Motivation

And the last one, that fits the "having a reason to do it".

First off, there are 2 types of motivation: instrinsic and extrinsic. You can think of them like pressures, like the ones that keep stars burning without crushing themselves or forming supernovas.

The intrinsic motivation is whatever you will feel like doing naturally without any pressure. This includes hobbies, like drawing or programing, painting, building a treehouse, wtvr anything that doesn't come from a exterior "pressure". That is, this would be a "interior" pressure.

The extrinsic motivation is whatever you feel like doing because there is something outside of you pushing you towards doing it. This includes money, social expectations, perhaps productivity...

One last thing before I end this draft is that, if you spend too much time on extrinsic motivation, it can be hard to go back into the simpler, more quiet intrinsic motivation, but in my experience, the intrinsic motivation is much more valuable because it actively helps you achive your purpose, what you will naturally feel good doing, not because I'm saying this, but because it comes from "your interiour", yourself. 

In a sense, it comes from the same place where your other needs such as eating and sleeping come from, and as such, it's a human need, hence why so many feel so "sad"/depressed on non fulfilling jobs, because it all comes from extrinsic motivation, things from the outside, that simply don't come from your interior.
