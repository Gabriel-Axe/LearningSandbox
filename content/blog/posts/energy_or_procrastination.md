As I come home after another Muay Thai session feeling like I just ran through a iron man and that my vital energy is being sucked out of a tube by little goblins and doom scroll through reddit to be fed doomslop, I ask myself why the heck do I feel so tired.

I didn't had a terrible sleep, my training wasnt that intensive, I don't eat junk food... What's the issue here?

Then I went a small philosophical question to find out what energy really is.

If you had the pleasure bestow your eyes on 1 or 2 of my previous posts (like the one in [[procrastination]] or in [[systems]]), you may know that I'm a bit of a fanatic for productivity.

Here's what I currently know about, not just energy, but motivation as well, because often we use the 2 to mean the same thing, but they are as far as it can be.

# It's not just 1 thing but rather 3

The thing about motivation, is that we use it as a umbrela term for a lot of things that are sorta connected, but at the same time are not.

It is not uncommon to talk about motivation when you refer to 1 of these things:
- Being excited towards doing something
- Having a reason to do something
- Being "able" to do something

However, this is highly unhelpful, because it's the same as if you went to the hospital because of a broken bone and to respond to "where was it" you respond "in a member". Thanks, there are like, 4, can you be more specific or are you not feeling it?

Using motivation as a umbrela term is not the most effective way to work towards productivity, since like I showed, it can be used in 3 extremelly different and non overlapping meanings.

# Why you feel exhausted after conversasions

I don't believe there is such a thing as social energy. Not anymore.

The reason, I think, you become exhausted after a conversation is not because "silly me, I should have know, I'm a introvert after all", it's because you are either thinking too much, or you are in a ambient you don't really like. Let me explain.

As I said before, we use energy for, pratically anything you can think off, from running, to thinking, to painting... Anyhting that requires phisical, cognitive or creative effort. Ang guess what? Being social has 2 of those, specially when you are trying to be funny all the time like mi myself i.

So, besides the fact that you are using energy to think, of your arguments, of how to respond to the other person, of what to say about something that interests you, you are also, if you are not in a ambient that amuses you, actively draining your... I wouldnt't say energy, but just as you would quickly become bored in a rock show if you don't like rock, or big sounds, so would you in a conversation about a subject you don't find amusing or care at all.

But even if you were to have a conversation that you liked, you would still feel exhausted. Why? Again, you are using energy, to think and respond, and energy is used for everything, be it in your brain or in your shoes.

# Energy

In physics, energy is the fuel that allows some body to make a action, and so it is in productivity.

Normally we think of productivity in terms of discipline, but the problem with discipline is that, if you keep being tested, one time or the other you will hit a limit because you can't say "No", or even stop. Energy is the thing that allows you to stop, or do the hard thing. No energy, no doing.

In nature, energy is the capacity of something doing, the potential for it, and so it is for humans.

You can think of energy as the thing that cna change at what direction you are "going to".

Imagine a ball that is traveling through space at a speed of, lets say, x 30m/s, y 0m/s and z0m/s, and you apply energy to it, like fuel for example, making it so that it can change it's own velocity from 30, 0, 0 to 17, 14, 0.

Energy can help you do something, even when you don't feel like. It's the thing that can help you change your motion, that can save you from doomscrolling.

# Motivation

And the last one, that fits the "having a reason to do it".

First off, there are 2 types of motivation: instrinsic and extrinsic. You can think of them like pressures, like the ones that keep stars burning without crushing themselves or forming supernovas.

The intrinsic motivation is whatever you will feel like doing naturally without any pressure. This includes hobbies, like drawing or programing, painting, building a treehouse, wtvr anything that doesn't come from a exterior "pressure". That is, this would be a "interior" pressure.

The extrinsic motivation is whatever you feel like doing because there is something outside of you pushing you towards doing it. This includes money, social expectations, perhaps productivity...

One last thing before I end this draft is that, if you spend too much time on extrinsic motivation, it can be hard to go back into the simpler, more quiet intrinsic motivation, but in my experience, the intrinsic motivation is much more valuable because it actively helps you achive your purpose, what you will naturally feel good doing, not because I'm saying this, but because it comes from "your interiour", yourself. 

In a sense, it comes from the same place where your other needs such as eating and sleeping come from, and as such, it's a human need, hence why so many feel so "sad"/depressed on non fulfilling jobs, because it all comes from extrinsic motivation, things from the outside, that simply don't come from your interior.
