# What are Addictions

Addictions are nothing more then a habit who came out of control. The difference between liking coffee and being addicted to coffee is knowing when you had your 3rd cup for the day or are way past it.

Therefore, as with any habit, the most simple thing you can do to stop it is to never start it. And as you can see, I'm following that rule pretty well, given I never touched LoL or Valorant (and it's definetely not because I'm a Arch Linux User).

# Exceptions

The reason many of us fall back into addiction, is because of 2 things, or 3, maybe 3 things, and it's the same reasons we skip our [[systems]] (since systems are also habits, in a sense).

These 3 things are what I call "exceptions". In general, a exception is somehting that only happens in exceptional, **rare** moments. Things that couldn't be foreseen.

Exceptions is what I call the "excuses" we tell ourselves when engage in a addiction. After all, it's never "ah forget it, I'll just give up". It's always "this one is a exception".

Exception are those things that we say to ourselves to continue to be prisoners of a addiction.

This is the main cause of failure and combing back to vices. Exceptions.

If you can't identify and manage your exceptions, the chains of habit will take hold of you, and forever shall you struggle by the strength of addiction.

There are many exceptions of course, but all of them can fit into 3 things:
- Time
- Location
- Situational

And these 3 can fit into 1: **Convenience**.

## Time Exceptions

Given the time that it is now, it would be convenient to do X thing. Using time as a excuse for, in this case not starting.

- "Given that it's almost lunch, it would be convenient to play just a bit of Terraria, considering I can't do much productive things after a heavy meal." 
- "Given that it's past 20:30, I'll just play more and turn off 21:00 to go to sleep"

## Location Exceptions

Given where I am, it would be convenient to do X thing, given Y. Perhaps the most invisible of them all, using location as a excuse.

- "It's fine to eat that sweet. You are at a birthday party after all"
- "You haven't seen your friends for months, why not relax the rules about coffee a little?"

## Situational Exceptions

Given the situation I find myself in, it would be convenient to do X.

- "Given that I have worked all week, it would be convenient to just, relax this weekend and just play some Project Zomboid and eat some chocolate."
- (another example)

# Game Specific Advice

All games are made equal, but not all games are equal.

Even when every game is composed of the same things: bits and buts with instructions to your CPU, what they send is radically different.

In case I didn't made myself clear, some games are more addicting then others. Perhaps that's way too obvious, but a curious quality of obviality is that generally it flies right past our head.

## Qualities of Game Addiction

There are around 4 or 5 ways in which a game can be more or less addictive.

### Information

How much information the game presents you, how much the game stimulates or overload your senses. 

Games with way, **way** too much things to do, either by what you need to do or what you can do, fall heavily in this category.

Examples include Factorio and Rimworld, or any stock market game that has a fishing minigame.

### Fairness

How much luck based is the game. 

Games that are completely skill based are much easier to turn off then luck based games. 

2 bad exameples are Rimworld and Terraria.

### Finite

Does the game has a end, or it can last forever? 

Games that have an end are games like, Sonic 2, Metal Gear Solid 3, Poly Bridge, Outer Wilds... You can techinically, play it forever, but after some point, you will have done everything that was possible to do. Be it in the normal way, the glitch way, the speedrun way, or any other fancy way.

Some bad examples are Project Zomboid and Rimworld.

### Modding

How much open is the game for modding and adding new content? The least important of all factors toward addictions, generally it's just the sprinkle on top that makes the game glue your eyes to the screen.

Some bad examples are Rimworld and Terraria.

### Setup

How much work you need to do to reach the *fun* part. 

Generally, most games are desigend in a way that the work is the price for the fun. There are, however, games where the struggle itself is part of the game. These are the most addicting of them all.

One such example is Rimworld, and perhaps Battle Brothers

### Openess

How much free to are you in the game? 

Are you pushed towards finishing the final quest, or can you live a relatively normal life in the middle of a zombie apocalypse?

One such example is Project Zomboid.

## Examples

Bellow, I'll list some of the most addicting games I have come across, and try to explain why.

I won't show their classification, because it's kinda noisy with a bunch of numbers, but I hope the description is enough to pass the message.

### Terraria 

The gold standard for modding. 

Not only that, but it's one of the most luck based games with no loot boxes or cassino mechanics. 

If you ever tried to find a icerang, get a bloodmoon without that crystal or even fight the slime king with no slime crown (it's hard to find rubies), you know what I'm talking about. 

But sometimes, the game goes the complete opposite way, like when I found 2 pinkies one after the other (it's a 1% spawn rate enemy).

### Project Zomboid

This is the kinda game that is basicly infinite. 

The only end in the game is either dying or drinking a bottle of bleach, which makes you dead. 

And it's also one of the most open games I ever played. You can exercise, plant, construct a base in the middle of the woods, go foraging, collect water from the rain, cook, do car mechanics... the list is endless. 

And the developers haven't even finished it yet.

### Factorio

Many call this digital crack. And I agree.

Factorio is a factory game. It's the most fair out of this list, and it's also the one with the most work to get into the "fun" part, but once you get there, you are hooked.

### Rimworld 

The reason I ever started to study what makes a game addicting in the first place. 

Not only is as moddable as Terraria (seriously, this game has one of the best modding communities of the entire game industry), but the struggle itself is part of the fun. 

And the game is like real life itself, read: unfair as heck. But when you optimize it and learn it's systems it is such a blast. 

This is, in my opnion, both the golden standard in making a game fun, and make a game addicting. This game is a drug.
