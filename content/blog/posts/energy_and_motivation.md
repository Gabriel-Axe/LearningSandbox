# There is a lot more to Motivation then just Energy

It's not uncommon to use motivation umbrela term for a lot of things that are kinda connected, but are not the same thing:
- Being excited towards doing something
- Having a reason to do something
- Being "able" to do something

But this is highly unhelpful. Imagine if you go to the doctor and to his question of "what are you feeling", you said "I'm feeling pain in a member". Thanks, there are like, 4, can you be more specific or are you not feeling it?

you said "a general pain in the upper body". He would take off your gloves and slap you. Or maybe not, pain in that location may be a heart attack...

When you use motivation as a umbrela term, you are not able to truly say what is the issue. Are you not able to get off the bed? Don't you see any reason on why you should do something? Or does this simply seems boring to you?

# Energy is for Potential

I don't believe there is such a thing as social energy. Not anymore.

It is anecdotal, but I think the reason we become "exhausted" after a conversation is not because "silly me, I should have know, I'm a introvert after all", it's because the same energy we use for exercise and running, we use for intelectual activities, as well as being remembering things and being conversational, not being a "uhu", "humm...", "yes" machine (like I am).

Energy in physics is defined as the fuel that allows some body to make a action, and the same is true in productivity.

Normally we think of productivity in terms of discipline, but the problem with discipline is that, if you keep being tested, there will be a time you will hit a limit, because you can't say "No", or even stop. Unless you are Goggins. Goggins doesn't need energy, energy needs David Goggins. 

To be clear, energy is the thing that allows you to stop, or do the hard thing. No energy, no doing.

Like in nature where energy is the potential to do things, so it is for humans.

Think of energy as the thing that allows you to change the direction you are going, your fuel.

It is energy can help you do something, even when you don't feel like. It's the thing that can help you change your motion, that saves you from doomscrolling.

# Motivation is for Why

Let's get started with this: there is 2 types of motivation: instrinsic and extrinsic.

You can think of them like the pressures that keep stars burning without crushing themselves or forming supernovas.

Intrinsic motivation is whatever you will feel like doing naturally, without anything pushing you, a "internal pressure" a need, just like eating or sleeping. In other words, hobbies, like drawing or programing, painting, building a treehouse, whatever that you aren't being "forced" or "pushed" to do.

Now, for the "bad" motivation, extrinsic motivation is whatever you feel like doing because there is something outside of you pushing you towards doing it. This includes money, social expectations, peer presure, being good at something, productivity...

Now, my knowledge ends here, and everything at this point is speculation, theories and analogies, but in my experience, intrinsic motivation is sorta like a cat. If you pursue it, you will make it run away. If you are too noisy, it will flee to somewhere quieter. If you try to bribe it, it will ignore you. And if you ignore it, only giving attention to the extrinsic doggy, it will never show up.

You can't create intrinsic motivation, and that is a issue, since it is the intrinsic motivation that keeps you moving without having to jump off a bridge out of sheer stress, it brings you happiness, it keeps the burnout boogeyman away. And many times, it is also what brings the thing that brings food on the table, you know, the "work your love" or whatever? Becoming better for the sake of being better and becoming better as a professional?

So, what do we do?

The answer is, I simply don't know. All I can say to you is what I'm trying.

For what I understand, you can't do these things to intrinsic motivation:
- Bribing
- Ignoring
- Chasing
- Overwhelm

For what I understand, there is only one thing you can do to get intrinsic motivation: nothing.

Exactly, nothing.

The good ol' putting down your phone and staring at a wall.

Why? I don't know.

My best theory is that the brain is a ever emotion/memory-processing machine, and to keep intrinsic motivation high, you gotta keep that machine flowing just like you don't want a factory machinery stopping production.

And the only way to keep this machine running is to have peaceful, quiet moments. No typing, no looking at funny memes, no listening to music, no reading, nothing. Stare at the sky and enjoy the clouds because that is the only thing you can do.

# Mood is for Vibes

Whanever productivity gurus say that "you don't need motivation", what they usually mean is "being excited to do something" or "feeling good while doing it" and in a sense, I agree.

But just because if is "less important" then the others, it doesn't mean it is not important. Someone who is happy about their work is more intrinsically determined to do it well then someone who just want to get it done.

Now, I'm not sure about this, but I feel like mood partly if not entirely from intrinsic motivation or a combination of both types of motivation, however motivation can still exist without a happy mood.

The issue with that is that the shortest path to somewhere is a straight line, but it's not uncommon to the line be harder then a zig-zag or a loop.

And the hard truth about hardship is that many times, it doesn't feel like it matter. Emphasis on "feels".

The problem is, as said before with intrinsic motivation, if you don't get enough of it, or in the case of doing something hard, if you don't get enough "feedback", proof that your hard work is leading somewhere, eventually you are gonna crash out, and that can create a loop where your lack of feedback or proof of work doesn't exist, which leads to poor motivation, which leads to poor enthusiasm, which leads to poor work and no proof of work and no motivation...

In short, mood is at the bottom of hte list between these 3 when it comes to productivity, but it's still important, since a extremelly poor mood can crush any motivation and suck out your energy. In a sense, mood is a amplifier.
